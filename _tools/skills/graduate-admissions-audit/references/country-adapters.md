# Country Adapters

Use the relevant section before final assessment. Apply the country-specific checks in addition to the generic workflow.

## Hong Kong

Mandatory checks:

- program-specific page vs university generic page
- local vs non-local tuition
- Chinese, English, or bilingual requirement
- interview, written test, portfolio, or Cantonese requirement
- round-based deadlines for competitive business or media programs

Common failure modes:

- applying university-wide IELTS minimum to all programs
- ignoring Chinese proficiency requirements
- missing local vs non-local fee gaps
- over-trusting non-local case data for local applicants

Confidence note:

- Public local-applicant case data is often thin. Lower confidence when community evidence is mostly non-local.

## United Kingdom

Mandatory checks:

- China-specific institution list or banding
- exact academic requirement wording for Chinese universities
- separate academic requirement from English requirement
- rolling vs fixed deadline behavior
- pre-sessional English or waiver options

Common failure modes:

- ignoring the university or department list for Chinese institutions
- reading a general 2:1 requirement without checking country-specific equivalency
- treating late-cycle applications as if they had the same chance as early-cycle applications

Confidence note:

- Timing matters strongly for rolling programs. A good applicant may still face weaker odds late in the cycle.

## Canada

Mandatory checks:

- four-year degree or equivalent requirement
- prerequisite courses at transcript level
- thesis, supervisor, or research-stream requirements
- reference letter and statement requirements
- domestic vs international tuition if both exist

Common failure modes:

- treating program title match as sufficient without checking prerequisites
- ignoring supervisor contact expectations
- assuming taught master's programs never require course-level preparation

Confidence note:

- If transcript details are missing, prerequisite judgement should usually be low confidence.

## Australia

Mandatory checks:

- degree equivalency and non-standard pathway acceptance
- domestic vs international tuition
- language package, waiver, or pre-sessional pathway
- intake frequency and quota behavior
- program page vs international admissions page consistency

Common failure modes:

- ignoring packaged English pathways
- assuming one intake pattern applies to the whole university
- missing quota or capacity notes on competitive programs

Confidence note:

- Rules are often structured, but intake timing and packaged language options can materially change recommendations.

## Cross-Country Reminders

- Keep the canonical fee fields stable even if the local terminology differs.
- Store the original country-specific wording in `raw_snippet`.
- When a country-specific rule blocks reliable automation, push it to manual review instead of smoothing it over.
